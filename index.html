Fleet control & navigation

Click a friendly vessel or drag a selection box. Hold Shift to add to your selection. Right-click water to move, or a detected enemy to attack. Drag with the middle or right mouse button to pan. Scroll to zoom; click the minimap to navigate.

Ctrl+A selects all. Ctrl+1–9 stores groups; 1–9 recalls them. F focuses your selection, Home fits the theatre, Space pauses, M moves, A attacks, S stops, B lays mines, N sweeps, and Esc cancels an active command or clears the selection.

Map and live information

[ toggles your fleet sidebar, ] toggles command controls, and \ maximizes or restores the map. H hides or shows map overlays. The view toolbar stays available in every layout. E opens the event log, C detected contacts, and O mission objectives. These live windows do not pause the battle or change speed. Press the same shortcut or its close button to dismiss a window; Esc closes the front window first. Tab navigates controls normally. Keys typed in a window or form never issue ship orders.

One vessel = manual weapons

Selecting one vessel disables its automatic offense AND defense. Choose a weapon in the fire-control panel, then click a target to fire once. You also control air defense, close-in interception and aircraft sorties. Clear your selection or select multiple vessels to restore autonomous engagement.

Selecting or switching vessels preserves their orders, routes and motion. Navigation follows the current route until you issue a new movement or Stop order. Ships turn and brake gradually; hulls avoid land and other vessels. At an ordered destination, ships may make a limited local defensive movement and then return to station. This does not enable automatic weapons on a selected vessel.

Ammunition & detection

Every weapon has a target class, effective range, cooldown in simulation seconds and finite stock. Offensive missile mounts fire limited salvos, then wait for the next engagement cycle; this delay is a fire-control decision, not a physical reload. SAMs engage aircraft and missiles; CIWS handles close threats; sonar reveals submarines. Surviving aircraft return to their carrier and consume finite stores to rearm. Radio silence reduces exposure and limits radar and communications.

Deployments begin paused at 1× so you can inspect your fleet and issue orders. Choose Start operation when ready. Choose any speed from 1× to 60× during transit or combat. Contacts and incoming attacks never change your selected speed. Scroll in for detailed ship views up to 960 px/km. Space pauses whenever you need time to plan.

Submarine depth

Select one friendly submarine and open Command (]). Enter a target depth in meters and choose Apply. 0 m orders the surface; each class has its own maximum. Current depth, ordered depth and diving or surfacing status update live. Depth changes gradually in simulation time and never replace a route or movement order. Pausing freezes the dive as well as navigation.

Collision checks use both the chart position and the vertical hull extent. A sufficiently deep submarine can pass beneath a surface vessel. Surfaced and shallow submarines can still collide with surface hulls; submarines at overlapping depths can collide with one another. Depth changes are blocked when another hull prevents safe passage. Islands remain solid at every depth. Exposed submarines can be found by radar and attacked by surface weapons. Fully submerged hulls require torpedoes or antisubmarine weapons; sonar still applies. Missile launches require a shallow depth; WWII torpedoes require periscope depth. Fire control explains any depth restriction. Enemy submarines choose depths for attacks and protection. Exact enemy depth is not displayed.

Mines, ASW and shore support

Select a minelayer and choose Lay mines (B), then click water. Before starting, place a field within 18 km of that vessel and in your own half of the theatre. Each field uses up to eight mines from finite stores. Once underway, the vessel travels to the ordered area and lays mines over time. Select a minesweeper and choose Sweep (N), then mark water to search and clear. Sweepers detect nearby hostile mines; a sweep order is required to remove them. Known fields appear in Contacts. Mines target hostile surface vessels; friendly passage is abstracted as chart-aware navigation.

Ships with ASW aircraft show an Antisubmarine sortie option in fire control. Select that aircraft weapon and a detected submarine to launch it. Aircraft availability depends on the fleet and era. Coastal batteries appear only in shoreline theatres: gun batteries in WWII, and guns or missiles in modern scenarios. They are fixed positions; select a battery to inspect and direct its available weapons.

Eras & objectives

WWII uses naval guns, straight-running torpedoes, flak and propeller aircraft. Modern warfare adds guided missiles and modern sensors. Encounters require a 75% loss of enemy combat strength, weighted by ship class. Port missions depend on the port objective. Island control requires 30 minutes of total control time; convoy missions require at least 60% of transports to escape. Choose a 2–24 hour operation duration in the briefing. The mission clock shows the remaining time and saved operations preserve that time limit. A destroyed ship becomes inactive, sinks gradually, then disappears.

Fuel is not modeled. Public specifications inform the equipment; damage, sensing, fleet composition and tactics are game abstractions.
